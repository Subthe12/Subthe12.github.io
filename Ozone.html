Ozone Depletion

Introduction

The Ozone Layer lies in the stratosphere between 20 and 40km above the Earth's surface. This layer protects the Earth from the ultraviolet rays of the Sun. It is essential to p preserve life on the Earth.


Effects of Ozone Depletion

Affect in Human Health

When the ozone layer becomes thin, it allows UV-B rays from the sun to reach the ground. These UV rays harm our bodies by damaging the DNA in our skin cells, which can lead to skin cancer. Additionally, these harmful rays damage our eyes and cloud the clear parts of the eye lens. This can result in a serious eye condition known as cataracts, which can lead to complete blindness.

Damage to Ocean Life

These UV rays penetrate the surface of the ocean and kill tiny floating sea plants called phytoplankton. These plants need sunlight at the surface to survive. Since they are the primary food source for small fish and sea insects, their destruction leads to a large-scale starvation issue. his, in turn, leaves larger fish, whales, and dolphins without food, and prevents the ocean from absorbing gases that contribute to global warming.

Prevention

Planting More Trees

It will improve the level of oxygen in the atmosphere and decrease the level of oxygen in the atmosphere and decreases the level of carbon dioxide.
